**The defect hunt is the most valuable artefact in this whole session.** The agent spent from
17:42 to 19:54 — over two hours — on a bug where the entire far half of the arena rendered black.
Its own memory file records the resolution:

> **Root cause: a tilted perspective camera clips everything beyond its target plane.**
> With `camera.perspective({ position: [0,7.4,5], target: [0,0,0] })`, every render item whose
> world bounds sat past the target was silently clipped — the entire far half of the arena.
> Bricks, paddle and ball all vanished; primitives with large bounds (the rim, the floor) survived,
> which made it look like a geometry or instancing bug for far too long.
>
> **Fix: straight-down `camera.orthographic({ position: [0,12,ARENA_Z], target: [0,0,ARENA_Z],
> orthographicSize: 4.3 })`.**

How it was found — and this is the technique worth reusing: **bisect with screenshots, don't rebuild.**
Move one object through a series of Z values and screenshot each step. The 23 diagnostic frames
survive in `arcade/prism-break/captures/diag/` (`sweep-zneg26.png`, `probe-far.png`,
`m1-model-far.png`, …). The probe that cracked it: ball at `z=+2.6` renders, `z=-1.0` does not —
a boundary just past the origin, which is the signature of a cull against the camera's target plane.

Supporting discovery: **`app.pause()` does not stop the frame loop** — the app kept overwriting the
probe values before they rendered. `__AURA3D_LIVE_APPS__.pauseAll()` does stop it; if you also need
to hold a value, neutralise the node's own setter:

```js
node.setInstances = () => node
```

### 6.4 The unresolved engine bug — still live, and it will bite both remaining games

This is the most important technical finding in the whole session, and it is **not fixed**.

**The bug:** a tilted perspective camera silently clips every render item whose world bounds sit
beyond the camera's *target plane*. Primitives with large bounds survive, which makes it read as a
geometry or instancing bug rather than a camera bug — that misdirection is why it consumed
**1 hour 45 minutes and eleven builds**.

**How it was worked around** (not fixed). Prism Break now uses a straight-down orthographic camera
*plus* a compensating world offset:

```ts
// arcade/prism-break/src/main.ts:36-44
// ARENA_Z exists because of an engine bug, not a design choice. The compiled
// …half of the frustum. Remove ARENA_Z once the frustum tracks the camera.
const ARENA_Z = 4.2;
```

Every arena node and the camera itself are then positioned at `z = ARENA_Z` (lines 473, 485, 491,
532–533). The code comment explicitly flags this as a workaround to be removed once the engine is
fixed.

**Why this matters to you:** the defect was worked around **in application code only** — nothing in
`packages/` was changed. So:

- **Backspin** uses `camera.orthographic({ position: [0, 1.8, 16], target: [0, 1.8, 0], … })` and
  does **not** apply the `ARENA_Z` offset. Whether a side-on ortho camera with content spread ±20
  units in X hits the same clip is **untested**. Watch for it on your first successful capture.
- **Vector Nine**, still unwritten, will hit it too — it is a chase-camera 3D game, the exact
  configuration that triggers it.

**The cheap diagnostic, if the far half of your scene vanishes:** place one object at a series of Z
values, screenshot each, and find the boundary. That is how this was cracked, after five failed
fixes based on guessing at geometry. The harnesses survive in `arcade/tools/diag*.mjs`, and the 23
diagnostic frames are in `arcade/prism-break/captures/diag/` (`sweep-zneg26.png`, `probe-far.png`,
`m1-model-far.png`, …). Two probes in particular proved it: a ball at `z=+2.6` renders, the same
ball at `z=-1.0` does not — a cutoff just past the origin, which is the signature of a cull against
the camera's target plane rather than anything geometric.

**Consider fixing it properly** in the engine rather than propagating the offset into two more
games. That is a bigger decision than this report should make for you, but the cost of *not*
deciding is two more rounds of the same two-hour hunt.

### Traps to avoid repeating

- **Run builds serially.** Never two concurrent Vite builds on this machine — three concurrent builds killed the shell itself (fd exhaustion).
- **Verify every `meshy make`/background job by listing its output directory.** Status "completed" with exit 0 is not proof of output.
- **Use `/opt/homebrew/bin/node` + `--use-angle=metal` for Playwright**, not the managed node, not swiftshader.
- **Use the Grep *tool*, not shell grep** — shell `grep -rn dist/` hangs to SIGTERM (30–130 s), and shell grep has produced a false negative on a 734-line file.
- **Quote your globs.** `--include=*.d.ts` returns empty output with **exit 0** — it reads as "symbol absent".
- **Never wrap a build in `timeout`** — you will SIGTERM your own build and misread it as a crash.
- **Always `cd arcade/<app>` before `tsc`/`vite`** — the binaries live in the repo root's `node_modules/.bin`.
- **Cap the typecheck heap**: `NODE_OPTIONS=--max-old-space-size=1024`, or the sandbox SIGKILLs with exit 137 and no output.
- **`createGame` invokes the scene factory synchronously** — declare all state above it.
- **Bisect visually with screenshots** rather than rebuilding — this is how the two-hour camera bug was finally cracked.
- **Trust the evidence beacon's `assetIds` only after checking it against `aura.assets.json`.**
- **Check `withinVertexCeiling` yourself.** `shrink-glb.mjs` hard-stops at 6 simplify passes and only *reports* the ceiling — it never enforces it. `bsGate` (30,732 verts) and `bsTower` (24,216) were admitted over the 20,000 limit.
- **"Motorcycle"/"bike" is a wheel prior in Meshy.** Describe geometry ("hover speeder", "magnetic lift discs recessed into the underside"), not the nearest real-world noun. The first hoverbike came back a wheeled motorcycle.
