## 01-simple-geometry: major-aura3d-deficiency (Aura 4.5 / three 7.5)

The image viewer returned empty results for all four PNGs, so I judged the frames from pixel data instead: per-pixel classification maps, column profiles and region statistics computed with PIL and numpy. Composition matches. In both frames the #1d2129 background (29,33,41) fills the top third, a grey ground plane fills the rest, and the red box, green sphere and blue cylinder sit at identical screen positions with matching silhouettes. Both engines render the sphere's specular highlight in the same place. The difference is the shadows. In three.js the box, sphere and cylinder each cast a crisp, near-black contact shadow (luma about 19-27 on ground of about 127) that extends to the left of and in front of the object bases, which grounds the objects firmly. In Aura3D the shadows land in roughly the same areas but are only a faint tint (about 122-124 on ground of 134-137, roughly 10% darker), so the objects look like they are floating. Unlit faces also look washed out in Aura3D: the shadowed side of the red box is RGB (95,2,7) against three's (54,1,2), and the green sphere's dark side is lifted the same way. The Aura3D ground is a few levels brighter (130-143 vs 127-132) and has a slight brightness gradient from far to near, while three's ground is flat. Lit object colours are close, with Aura3D about 5-10 levels brighter. The capability log records Aura3D's coarser primitive meshes (sphere 16x12, cylinder 24 segments, vs 64x32 and 48 in three.js). At a sphere diameter of about 145px that should cause slight silhouette faceting, but I could not confirm it visually.

**Differences:**
- Cast shadows are nearly invisible in Aura3D: shadowed ground is about 10% darker than lit ground, versus about 80% darker in three.js. — *defaults / shadow strength* — Production shadow strength defaults to 0.24-0.38, and the shadow label here is 'aura3d-root-production-product-1024px-shadow-map', which maps to about 0.24 for product scenes. It is applied as mix(1,1-occlusion,strength), and the public API has no intensity option. See research/04-lighting-ibl-shadows.md:13 and :272 (agent-api/index.ts:12966-12968, ShaderLibraryCore.ts:530).
- Shadowed and unlit faces are lifted: red box shadow side is 95,2,7 vs 54,1,2, and the green sphere's dark side is brighter, giving a flatter, washed-out look. — *ambient units* — Aura3D's ambient term has no 1/pi factor, so ambient 0.4 is about pi times three.js AmbientLight(0.4). See research/04:12 (ShaderLibraryCore.ts:622-634, ShaderChunks.ts:212).
- No IBL or environment response: lighting is a uniform ambient wash. — *ambient disables IBL* — The 'authored-ambient' early return sets environment intensity to 0 whenever lights.ambient is present. The report confirms environment.preset='authored-ambient' and iblPixelBacked=false. See research/04:11 (agent-api/index.ts:12701-12716).
- Lower primitive tessellation and a 1024px shadow map, vs the spec's 2048 with bias -0.0005 and normalBias 0.02. — *missing capability* — primitives.sphere and primitives.cylinder have no segment options (research/02:109, index.ts:17360-17362), and the public API exposes no shadow mapSize, bias or extent settings. Both gaps are recorded in the capability log as 'missing'.
- Aura3D ground and lit surfaces are slightly brighter (mean luma 90.1 vs 86.1). — *minor tone/exposure* — Extra ambient energy combined with the hard-coded exposure of 1 and ACES curve (diagnostics report a category exposure of 0.92 that is not applied). See research/05.

**Metrics:** The metrics overstate how close the two images are. SSIM is 0.984, PSNR 26.5 dB, MAD 4.4, and only 3.8% of pixels differ by more than 16. Roughly 35% of the frame is identical flat background, and most of the rest is flat ground that differs by only about 5 levels. The changed pixels are almost exactly the shadow regions and the shadow sides of the objects, which is where the scene's stated purpose ('one directional + ambient, shadow') is tested. ssimMinWindow is -0.046, which shows those local regions are structurally different. Visually the gap is much larger than SSIM 0.98 suggests.

**Harness fairness:** The comparison looks fair. Both sides translate the same SceneSpec (shared/scenes.ts:104-118): camera at [0,2.4,6], fov 45; ambient 0.4; sun intensity 3 at [4,7,3] with shadows; the same three primitives with the same materials, plus a 12-unit ground plane. The Aura3D side (aura3d/common.ts:148-201) uses the public API idiomatically: lights.ambient, lights.directional with shadow:true, primitives[shape] with castShadow and receiveShadow, .position and .rotate, and scene().background(color). I found no hidden tuning or sabotage, and the gaps are honestly recorded in the capability log. The three.js side (three/common.ts:69-76, 126, 172-206) uses PCFSoftShadowMap with the spec's 2048 map, bias and normalBias, ACES tone mapping, exposure 1 and sRGB output. That is the spec, not extra tuning. The only asymmetry is mesh tessellation and shadow configuration, which Aura3D cannot set through its public API, and the log flags both. Neither engine logged console errors, and both report status ready.

## 02-pbr-product: major-aura3d-deficiency (Aura 5 / three 7.5)

Caveat: the Read tool returned empty results for every PNG and JPEG crop, so I could not view the images directly. This description comes from pixel sampling of aura3d.png and three.png with PIL. Both frames have the same layout: a flat #2a2d33 background (luma 45) over the top ~60%, a grey ground plane, a large light plinth disc across the bottom third (x 405-875, rows ~540-719), and the antique camera, which is small (~150x280 px at x 545-765, rows 80-360) and sits at the top of the frame. That is only about 5% of the frame. The camera silhouettes match almost pixel for pixel: row extents agree within 1-3 px on every sampled row, so the GLB transform, scaleMode 'world', and camera framing are equivalent. The differences: (1) Grounding shadow. three.js casts a clearly dark shadow on the ground behind the plinth (luma 66 against lit ground at 104) and a visible camera shadow on the plinth top (185 against 217). In Aura3D the same ground pixels are ~101, so the ground shadow is essentially gone, and the plinth top shows little shadow-versus-lit contrast. (2) Plinth geometry. Aura3D's plinth top starts ~24 px lower on its far rim. At column 760, three.js reaches plinth white at row 556 and Aura3D only at row 580, and the pixels in between are ground-coloured (98,102,113), so the back of the disc is missing or foreshortened. The front edge and side extents match. (3) Plinth shading. three.js renders a uniform 216-217. Aura3D has a gradient from 177 at the back to 203 at the front, so the plinth reads darker and greyer than the #d9d9d6 albedo. (4) Camera body. Aura3D's object is about as good, but it looks to be floating on a slightly clipped, dimmer plinth.

**Metrics:** SSIM 0.969, PSNR 25.0, MAD 4.25, changedPixelRatio 8.4%, mean luma 76.6 vs 79.7. These metrics flatter Aura3D. About 54% of the frame is identical flat background colour, and the actual product (the camera) covers about 5% of the frame, where silhouettes match closely. The perceptually important failures are the missing grounding shadow and the clipped plinth rim. They sit inside the 8% of changed pixels and show up in ssimMinWindow = -0.42, but the global averages wash them out. A 0.97 SSIM should not be read as near-equivalence here.

**Harness fairness:** Fair. Both engines get the same spec from benchmarks/quality-rebuild/shared/scenes.ts:120-132: camera, sun(2,[3,5,2]), studioSmall08 HDRI at intensity 1, ground plane at y=-0.1, cylinder plinth [1.6,0.1,1.6], and the GLB at scale 0.25 rotated by -0.5. The Aura3D side (aura3d/common.ts:139-216) uses only public API: environments.hdri, lights.directional({shadow}), primitives.cylinder({size}).position, and model(asset,{scaleMode:'world'}).rotate/scale. It uses no hacks. scaleMode 'world' is the correct choice for parity, and it waits for the HDRI to reach the ready state. Gaps are logged honestly as missing rather than worked around: shadow-map-config, primitive tessellation, and tone-mapping selection. The three.js side (three/common.ts:69-79) uses higher-tessellation primitives and the spec's shadow settings, because those options exist there. This is a legitimate capability gap, not harness bias. Small notes: PCFSoftShadowMap is deprecated in r185 and silently becomes PCFShadowMap (console warning), which slightly favours neither engine. The three.js run logged an ERR_ABORTED request for antique-camera.glb, but the model rendered and status is ready, so it was likely a cancelled duplicate fetch and is harmless. I could not view the PNGs directly (Read returned empty), so this assessment rests on pixel-level sampling.

## 03-damaged-helmet: minor-aura3d-deficiency (Aura 6.5 / three 8.5)

The image viewer returned empty content for all four PNGs, and for JPEG/cropped conversions too, so I couldn't inspect them by eye. This description comes from a per-pixel analysis of aura3d.png and three.png with PIL/numpy, so read it with that caveat. Both images are 1280x720, with the helmet on a #1a1d22 background and identical background pixels [26,29,34]. The subject bounding box matches exactly in both (x 339-807, y 122-653), covering about 17.8% of the frame, so camera, framing, model load and transform agree. Highlights match: in pixels three.js renders with luma above 150, mean luma is 197.4 for three.js and 198.7 for Aura3D. Visor/cyan pixel counts are 690 vs 692. Aura3D's shadows and midtones are clearly lifted. Pixels that are dark in three.js (luma 0-15, mean 9.0) average 20.5 in Aura3D, and the 15-40 band goes from 26.1 to 38.5. Subject p10 luma is 11.1 for three.js and 19.8 for Aura3D. The top of the helmet is about 11 luma brighter (69.6 to 80.7). Aura3D's subject also has slightly more clipped pixels (1.58% vs 1.09% at or above 250) and a few more warm saturated pixels near the emissive panels (19 vs 3). The result is that the Aura3D helmet looks flatter and lower in contrast, with crevices, panel seams and AO-darkened areas less deep. three.js keeps the deeper occlusion and darker metal you'd expect from a scene lit only by an HDRI. Edge gradient energy is similar (14.1 vs 12.9), so neither image shows a sharpness or AA problem.

## 04-clearcoat: minor-aura3d-deficiency (Aura 6 / three 8)

Column A (base, no coat) is clearly darker in Aura3D: red sphere RGB 188,47,49 vs 216,74,66 in three; blue spheres about 40,50,100 vs about 53,65,130, so base albedo/IBL response is roughly 20-25% darker. Aura3D's column A highlights are also hotter and harder (max luma about 232 vs 191, higher std), which points to a sharper/stronger direct specular. Caveat: the Read tool returned no viewable content for any of the four PNGs (aura3d.png, three.png, side-by-side.png, diff.png), so this judgment comes from pixel analysis rather than looking at the images. I used region means, ASCII luminance maps, blob detection, ground-shadow segmentation and gradient energy on both 1280x720 captures. Both frames have the same composition. The upper ~40% is flat sky/fog. Below it is a green ground plane, the soldier at x510-830/y360-635 in both, the crate stack left of center, the rock and props, and three emissive cyan pickups whose bright cores sit at the same pixel centers in both (763,335), (632,355), (701,395). So geometry, camera, asset placement and the animated pose agree. The differences are in lighting quality. (1) Player shadow: three.js shows a clear soldier/prop shadow on the ground, about 8,600 ground pixels more than 12 luma below the row median, with a dip of about 30 luma at row 560. In Aura3D that drops to about 500 pixels with no measurable dip at the same spot, so the character barely looks grounded. (2) Aura3D is darker and muddier overall: mean luma 144 vs 158, near ground 107,122,80 vs 129,145,100, soldier region about 15 levels darker. (3) The lit face of the left crate stack is much dimmer in Aura3D (about 150-169 vs about 178-200; three.js has a 4,200 px highlight area that Aura3D lacks). (4) Pickup bloom cores are about 1.5-2x larger in Aura3D (1253/1781/2990 px vs 661/970/1936) at the same white-cyan color (about 220,242,244 in both), so the glow is softer and spreads more. **Differences:**
- Soldier/prop cast shadow on the ground is almost invisible in Aura3D (about 500 vs 8,620 darkened ground pixels) even though the shadow map is rendered and sampled — *shadow strength / fit* — Directional shadow strength hard-defaulted to 0.32, a whole-scene ortho fit over the 60 m ground (lightMatrix scale ~0.06-0.12, so roughly ±8-17 m instead of the spec's 14 m extent), no normal bias, and a nearest-sampled grid PCF (research/04-lighting-ibl-shadows.md:18, :56, :264-274, :330; agent-api/index.ts:12959-13000; Renderer.ts:1964-1986). There is no public shadow mapSize/bias/extent/intensity option (capability log 'shadow-map-config: missing').
- Whole frame about 14 luma darker; ground and character noticeably muddier — *exposure / tone pipeline* — Exposure is hard-coded to 1 with no public control, while diagnostics claim a 'product' preset of 0.92 that is not applied (research/05-postfx-aa-tonemap-color.md:26, :166-177). Weak shadows plus the dimmer direct/IBL balance mean lit surfaces never reach three.js levels. three.js also tone-maps its background through OutputPass, which partly accounts for the gap in the sky region.
- Left crate stack's lit face much dimmer and lower contrast — *PBR/IBL response* — Differences in IBL/specular response for the textured crate GLB (research/03-pbr-materials-gltf.md, research/04 PMREM/IBL sections). - Pickup bloom halos about 1.5-2x larger and softer — *bloom* — Engine-specific bloom radius/threshold units, a soft-knee convention that blooms mid-tones, and antiBlowout clamping (research/05-postfx-aa-tonemap-color.md:24; capability log 'bloom: partial').
- Slightly softer edges and faceted primitives — *AA / tessellation* — Fixed primitive tessellation (sphere 16x12, cylinder 24) versus three.js 64x32/48 ('primitive-tessellation: missing'), plus blur-style AA at low DPR (research/05).
- Fog caps at 0.80 opacity in Aura3D — *fog* — effects.fog() caps opacity at 0.25+0.55*intensity. Barely visible here because density 0.03 at 5-15 m only gives about 2-18% fog.

**Metrics:** SSIM 0.965, PSNR 23.8 dB, MAD 14.1, changed-pixel ratio 0.44. The high SSIM misleads. The metrics do not show the missing shadow.

**Harness fairness:** Fair, and Aura3D is authored idiomatically. Every gap goes to the capability log rather than being hidden. three/common.ts applies the spec's shadow extent, bias and normalBias (:203-214), which Aura3D cannot express. That is a real engine API gap, not harness bias. Small asymmetries: three.js goes through EffectComposer + OutputPass, which tone-maps the background clear color, so its sky is brighter. three.js also logs that PCFSoftShadowMap is deprecated and fell back to PCFShadowMap. Both runs were 'ready' with no page errors. The ERR_ABORTED asset requests on both sides look like duplicate preloads being cancelled, since all 7 Aura3D assets report 'ready' and the three.js animation was applied.
